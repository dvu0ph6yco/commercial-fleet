## Field Notes

During a factory audit, do not stop at the showroom. Inspect the welding area, machining accuracy, paint line, assembly floor, and finished-vehicle test zone. Check whether technicians follow documented procedures and whether serial numbers are matched to records. If the supplier cannot clearly show how they manage non-conforming parts, the risk of inconsistent production is high.

Also, evaluate communication quality. Good exporters answer technical questions directly, provide drawings, and confirm details such as chassis wheelbase, PTO matching, outrigger span, and emergency lowering functions. Poor communication before the sale usually becomes worse after shipment.

## FAQ

**Q: are empty propane tanks hazardous waste**

Empty propane tanks should not be treated as ordinary trash. Even when “empty,” propane cylinders may still contain residual gas or pressure, so they can be hazardous if damaged, punctured, or improperly handled. In most cases, they are managed as regulated waste or returned for refill/recycling, depending on local regulations and the tank’s condition. If the tank is in good shape, the preferred option is usually to take it to an approved propane supplier, recycling center, or hazardous waste facility. For disposal or transport, always follow local safety and environmental rules and never cut, crush, or incinerate the tank.
